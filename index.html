    01

    Why Industrial & Energy deserves its own motion

    The largest energy companies have put hard dollar targets on structural cost, and they are consolidating engineering and IT into a few hubs to hit them. That is a buying condition for autonomous engineering capacity.

    $20BExxonMobil structural cost savings target by 2030, with $15.1B delivered since 2019
    $3–4BChevron structural cost reduction target by end of 2026, about $1B of it from technology
    $299MHalliburton severance charges in FY2025 as it cut headcount

    What makes this a distinct ICP

    A vertical is only worth building if the buyer, the pain, and the proof differ from the horizontal motion. In Industrial & Energy, three things do:

    Legacy depth

    Decades of plant, field, and back-office applications on old stacks, plus large ERP migrations already underway (ExxonMobil is moving from SAP ECC6 to S/4HANA). Modernization is a budgeted program, not a wish.

    Governance is the product

    Safety-critical operations mean every change needs review, audit, and controls. Devin's enterprise tiers, including single-tenant VPC and customer-managed keys, are the reason a security team says yes.

    Knowledge walking out the door

    Workforce reductions and retirements are removing the people who understand these systems. An agent that documents and works the codebase (DeepWiki, Devin) keeps that capacity in-house.

    Proof Cognition can already point to

    There is no published Devin customer in energy yet, which is the opening. The closest industrial and regulated references:

    • Mercedes-Benz cut a COBOL migration from about 8 months to 8 days.
    • GE Aerospace is a published case study.
    • Nubank reported 8–12x engineering time efficiency on an ETL migration.
    • Itaú uses Devin to remediate about 70% of security vulnerabilities automatically.

    02

    The ideal customer profile, built from the product out

    Most ICPs start with firmographics and work back to pain. This one starts with what only Cognition does and follows it outward to the account.

    1. DifferentiatorsWhat only we do
    • Devin is an autonomous engineer: it takes a ticket to a pull request, asynchronously and in parallel. Copilots assist one developer at a time.
    • Cloud agents (Devin) and local agents (Windsurf) in one platform, routed to whichever model fits the task.
    • Enterprise deployment options from isolated multi-tenant to single-tenant VPC with customer-managed keys; SOC 2 Type II; no training on customer code by default.
    • The value is new engineering capacity, not faster typing.
    2. Pain pointsWhat the customer feels
    • Hard cost targets with fewer engineers to deliver them.
    • Legacy estates that absorb budget and people.
    • Security and compliance remediation backlogs that keep growing.
    • System knowledge leaving with retirements and reductions.
    3. Use casesWhere the pain shows up
    • ERP and application migrations, such as SAP ECC6 to S/4HANA custom code.
    • Framework upgrades and legacy language modernization.
    • Vulnerability remediation and test coverage.
    • Integration and data pipelines between field and plant systems and the enterprise.
    • Product engineering for commercial energy software (Halliburton's Landmark).
    4. PersonasWho owns those use cases
    • Champion: the engineering hub or platform engineering leader who owns the backlog.
    • Strategic sponsor: the executive accountable for the cost or AI program.
    • Economic buyer: the CIO or chief technology officer, with the CISO as co-sponsor for remediation.

    Chapter 05 maps these by account type.

    5. CompaniesWhere those personas sit

    Energy and industrial companies with a large internal software organization, a deep legacy estate, a public cost or AI mandate, and an engineering hub already consolidating the work.

    Qualifying signals: a published savings target, an active modernization program, a central engineering or shared-services hub, and prior GenAI purchases.

    From the book

    Build the ICP from the product outward

    Five questions, in order:

    1. What are the major differentiators of the product?
    2. What pain points does it solve for customers?
    3. In which use cases do those pain points emerge?
    4. Which personas own those use cases?
    5. In which companies are those personas and use cases located?

    At Cognition

    The Industrial & Energy ICP

    I've answered all five for Devin in this vertical (chapter 02). The order matters: it forces account selection to follow real use cases, which is why ExxonMobil and Chevron lead on modernization and hub consolidation, and Halliburton leads on product engineering.

    Target accounts

    Four Houston-area accounts, prioritized by how many ICP signals are public today. Gaps are marked so discovery starts in the right place.

    ExxonMobil

    Tier 1

    Spring, TX. $332.2B revenue (FY2025). 58,000 employees.

    What they've said

    • $20B structural savings by 2030; $3.0B delivered in 2025 alone.
    • Enterprise AI and process automation run through Global Business Solutions (GBS).
    • Simplifying a complex IT estate; moving SAP ECC6 to S/4HANA.
    • About 2,000 roles cut in 2025, consolidating into regional hubs.

    Where Devin fits

    • S/4HANA custom-code remediation and migration.
    • Capacity for the Bengaluru Technology Center (900+ technologists).
    • Remediation backlogs across a shared IT service.

    Entry personas

    • GBS leadership and process transformation (John DiTullio).
    • Neil Hansen, now CFO, ran GBS through January 2026.

    To find: the current GBS president and whoever owns IT or the AI program. No CIO is publicly named.

    Chevron

    Tier 1

    Houston, TX. $189.0B revenue (FY2025). 43,039 employees.

    What they've said

    • $3–4B structural cost reduction by end of 2026, about $1B from technology.
    • Moving finance, HR, and IT to service centers in Manila and Buenos Aires.
    • Up to 20% workforce reduction by end of 2026.
    • Already buys GenAI: Microsoft Copilot, Claude, and in-house tools, plus an AI "moonshot" program.

    Where Devin fits

    • Engineering capacity for the Bengaluru ENGINE center (opened October 2025, 1,000+ hires).
    • Hess integration work, now targeting $1.5B in synergies.
    • Holding delivery steady through the workforce reduction.

    Entry personas

    • T. Ryder Booth, Chief Technology & Engineering Officer (IT is in his remit).
    • Jeff Gustavson, CFO-designate from January 2027 and an AI strategy owner.

    To verify: whether Bill Braun is still CIO. Leadership is reshuffling ahead of CEO succession, so timing matters.

    Halliburton

    Tier 1

    Houston, TX. $22.2B revenue (FY2025). 46,000+ employees.

    What they've said

    • Sells software commercially through Landmark (DecisionSpace 365), so it runs a real product engineering organization.
    • Buying more software: Sekal (drilling automation) and InformatiQ (cloud-native asset management) in 2026.
    • CEO Jeff Miller frames AI around repetitive tasks and faster execution.
    • $299M severance in FY2025.

    Where Devin fits

    • Integrating acquired codebases into Landmark.
    • Product engineering velocity on DecisionSpace 365, LOGIX, and ZEUS IQ.
    • Internal IT, separately, as a second entry point.

    Entry personas

    • Tony Antoun, SVP Landmark Software and Services.
    • Shannon Slocum, COO, whose remit includes global technology.

    To find: the CIO, who is described in the 10-K but not named.

    Enterprise Products Partners

    Tier 2

    Houston, TX. $52.6B revenue (FY2025). Staff are employed by its affiliate EPCO.

    What they've said

    • No public digital, IT, or AI strategy found.
    • Growth capex guided down to $1.9–2.3B for 2026, from $4.4B in 2025.
    • Co-CEO Jim Teague retires January 2027; Randy Fowler becomes sole CEO.

    Where Devin fits

    • To be discovered. Start with pipeline operations software and back-office systems.

    Entry personas

    • IT historically reported through finance (CFO R. Daniel Boss once held IT).
    • Graham Bacon, COO.

    Prospect from scratch: no public technology leader or partnerships. The CEO transition is a timing factor.

    06

    Day 1 to Day 90

    Three phases, each ending with something I can show: a map, a set of live conversations, and a first scoped deployment.

    Days 1–30Map the ground
    • Learn the Cognition sales motion, deal desk, and the reference customers closest to this vertical.
    • Fill the people gaps on all four accounts: the current owners of IT, engineering hubs, and AI programs.
    • Build a one-page point of view for each account, tied to its own published targets.
    • Meet the partner teams already working these accounts, starting with Cognition's systems integrator partners.
    Days 31–60Open the conversations
    • First meetings with champions at the three Tier 1 accounts.
    • Run discovery to one named use case per account, with an owner and a baseline.
    • Agree on security review requirements early, since governance decides the timeline.
    Days 61–90Land the first deployment
    • A scoped pilot in at least one account, with success measured against the baseline.
    • An expansion map from that use case to the next team.
    • The first energy reference story, which the vertical doesn't have yet.
